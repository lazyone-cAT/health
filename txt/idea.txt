PROBLEM DESCRIPTION

Data availability, accuracy, consistency, and reporting gaps limit timely and effective management decision-making across hospital operations.

Operations leads across the hospital are expected to make daily decisions on bed capacity, staffing response, and patient flow. The information they need to make those decisions exists — but not in one place, not in one shape, and rarely in time. What reaches them is assembled by hand, hours or days after the events it describes, and frequently disagrees with a report assembled by another department from the same underlying reality.

Six contributing factors have been observed:

1. Disparate sources
   Operational data is fragmented across unintegrated HIS, EMR, and standalone spreadsheets.

2. Manual compilation
   Significant staff time is spent manually collating daily operational reports.

3. Data inconsistency
   Conflicting metrics create different "versions of truth" across departmental reports.

4. Reporting delays
   Information reaches operational leads days after events, impeding quick response.

5. Limited real-time visibility
   No live dashboards exist for bed occupancy, queue status, or resource bottlenecks.

6. Administrative burden
   High reporting effort draws attention away from core operational management.

THE CHALLENGE QUESTION

Do decision-makers currently have the right information, at the right time, in the right format?

This is the question your submission has to answer. Everything you build should be traceable back to it.

TAT stands for Turnaround Time.

In the context of your hospital operations project, it specifically means:

The total time taken from when a lab test (like a blood test or X-ray) is ordered to when the result is reported back to the doctor or nurse.

Why is TAT so important for this project?
The sample data you are given includes a "Lab order-to-result turnaround log". Right now, the Operations Lead cannot see this metric in real-time. Here is why it matters:

It blocks bed flow: A patient cannot be discharged or moved to surgery until their lab results are back. If the average TAT is 4 hours but the hospital thinks it is 2 hours, they make bad decisions about admitting new patients.

It exposes hidden delays: The TAT is usually broken down into sub-steps (e.g., Collection Time ➔ Reception in Lab ➔ Processing ➔ Result Verification). If you build your dashboard and see that TAT is 5 hours, but the actual machine processing only takes 30 minutes, you immediately know the delay is in transport or collection—which is an operational problem you can fix.

How your app should use TAT:
In the Dashboard KPIs: Show "Current Average Lab TAT" (e.g., 2.5 hours) with a color. Green if under 2 hours, Red if over 4 hours.

In the Patient Timeline: Visually show the gap between the "Lab Order" timestamp and the "Result Returned" timestamp.

In Reconciliation: If the HIS system says a result was returned at 10:00 AM, but the Lab Log says 10:30 AM, your reconciliation rules must pick one (e.g., trust the Lab Log as the source of truth for TAT) and flag the 30-minute conflict.

In short: TAT is the heartbeat metric for operational efficiency in your app. Shorten the TAT, and you free up beds faster.
